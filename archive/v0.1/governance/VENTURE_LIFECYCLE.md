# Venture lifecycle and replication

Version 0.1 | Proposed

## Stages and evidence

| Stage | Required output | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Idea brief, customer, problem, intended outcome, constraints | Mandate authorizes discovery |
| Discovery | Demand evidence, alternatives, feasibility, dependency and cost estimates | Predefined validation thresholds met |
| Build | Working product, tests, operations plan, support and pricing model | Acceptance and assurance checks pass |
| Launch | Controlled production rollout, customer terms, monitoring, rollback | Launch scope authorized and checks pass |
| Operate | Real customer outcomes, maintenance, support, reconciled economics | Sustained viability criteria met |
| Graduate | Evidence packet and independent review | Every graduation gate passes |
| Replicate and transfer | Dedicated venture runtime and verified handover | New runtime operates and rollback is tested |
| Portfolio operation | Ongoing oversight, improvements, financial reporting | Continue, recover, pivot, or close |

Failed gates return the venture to an earlier stage, trigger a bounded pivot, or lead to closure. A new name or cloned runtime must not erase a failed venture's history.

## Viability

The board-approved mandate defines numeric thresholds and an observation window before launch. A venture is not viable merely because a demo works or revenue first appears.

Graduation evidence must establish:

- Demand: retained or repeat customers, or equivalent contracted demand appropriate to the model.
- Economics: collected revenue and credible contribution economics including model inference, compute, hardware, fulfillment, support, refunds, and shared operating costs.
- Liquidity: funded obligations and the required cash reserve after the transfer.
- Reliability: agreed service targets met over the observation window, including recovery exercises.
- Customer support: response and resolution targets met without routine board intervention.
- Autonomy: production operation sustained over the defined window, with every manual dependency measured and disclosed.
- Security and obligations: no unresolved issue above the permitted severity threshold; current asset, data, and contractual inventories.
- Transfer readiness: reproducible deployment, isolated access, staffed AI functions, validated runbooks, and operating budget.

No numeric thresholds are silently supplied by this document. Until thresholds, window, and portfolio limits are adopted, graduation remains a proposal for the board.

## Controlled replication

Replication is a business deployment event, not unrestricted self-propagation.

1. Verify graduation evidence and the active portfolio authorization.
2. Allocate a unique venture ID, budget, accounts or accounting boundaries, resource quotas, and accountable AI owner.
3. Deploy a pinned and verified operating-system version in an isolated environment.
4. Provision new least-privilege credentials; never copy parent secrets.
5. Transfer only authorized code, knowledge, data, and customer obligations, with provenance and access checks.
6. Reconcile asset ownership, finances, open tickets, inventory, suppliers, and service responsibilities.
7. Run acceptance checks and a monitored handover; keep the prior system recoverable.
8. Switch responsibility only after verification, then remove obsolete access.
9. Update the portfolio registry and release parent capacity for the next authorized idea.

Each descendant remains subject to board revocation and parent governance. Descendants may not spawn further ventures unless their mandate explicitly grants bounded replication authority. Global venture count, spending, compute, and replication-rate limits apply across the entire portfolio.

The parent must not leave an existing venture without support to pursue a new one.

## Pivot and closure

A pivot inside the existing purpose and limits may be autonomous. A broader mandate requires a board decision.

Pause acquisition and new commitments when predefined stop conditions are met. Prepare customer notices, refunds or fulfillment, data disposition, supplier termination, asset handling, and final reconciliation. Execute closure within delegated authority; reserve permanent parent-company closure for the board. Keep required records and document unresolved obligations.
