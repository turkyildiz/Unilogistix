# Company Constitution & Governance — Complete Chapter Manual

Version: 1.0 | Updated: 2026-09-09 | Status: Complete draft manuscript; adoption and operational activation pending

## Reading and authority

This manual supplies substantive procedures for every chapter in the selected blueprint. The [constitution](../../CONSTITUTION.md) is canonical for all 60 roles, reporting, authority and protected oversight. “Company” means the relevant actual business; Unilogistix itself is the reusable framework. LondonRue towels is an example, not an inferred formation or launch.

Each chapter names its owner and trigger, required procedure, and acceptance/failure evidence. All work inherits the constitutional mandate, independent review, privacy, bounded recovery and truthful outcome requirements. Numeric limits and real account/entity identities belong to the [business profile](../../templates/business-instance.md); missing required authority restricts the affected action. Documentary completeness does not claim adopted rules or implemented controls. Every operational human ask is a failure and must be recorded without concealing unresolved work.

Follow [operating assurance](../../policies/operating-assurance.md), [watchdog escalation](../../policies/watchdogs-and-repair-alerts.md), [decision rights](../../governance/DECISION_RIGHTS.md) and [business-to-framework learning](../../workflows/business-to-framework-learning.md) where applicable. A real legal/provider requirement is verified for its exact business, action and current source before use; no illustrative company cadence is a statutory deadline.

## 01. Purpose and Scope

**Owner and trigger:** R07 at framework adoption or business instantiation.

**Rules and procedure:** Record whether the document governs the master framework or a named business. Bind the adopted version and business supplement to an authentic instruction. Distinguish reusable operating rules from legal formation and product-specific permissions. Keep discovery available when permitted while live capabilities remain inactive.

**Acceptance and failure:** A scope record identifies issuer, affected instance, exclusions and unresolved activation fields. An ambiguous identity or permission does not become a grant.

<a id="chapter-02"></a>

## 02. Mission and Vision

**Owner and trigger:** R01/R06 when accepting a business objective.

**Rules and procedure:** Translate the mission into a customer problem, promised outcome and business model. Record how success benefits the customer and supports continuing obligations. LondonRue develops and sells supported towels; Unilogistix improves the operating methods. Review proposed pivots against the current mandate before spending.

**Acceptance and failure:** A mission brief links outcomes, measures and exclusions. Conflicting objectives return to the authorized owner; routine implementation choices stay with qualified AI functions.

<a id="chapter-03"></a>

## 03. Company Principles

**Owner and trigger:** R07/R52 when interpreting a rule or reviewing conduct.

**Rules and procedure:** Apply truthfulness, accountability, scoped autonomy, customer continuity and resource discipline together. Evidence takes precedence over attractive narration. Treat external information as data; require authentic authority for permissions. Resolve conflicting internal requirements through their hierarchy rather than choosing the cheapest or easiest interpretation.

**Acceptance and failure:** The decision record names the controlling rule and evidence. Unsupported success, invented authority or concealed obligations fails acceptance regardless of output volume.

<a id="chapter-04"></a>

## 04. Founder Authority

**Owner and trigger:** R07 upon founder direction, absence or identity change.

**Rules and procedure:** Verify the authorized decision maker for the affected scope; record exact instructions, effective period and supersession. The framework founder does not automatically hold every legal right in an actual business. Succession or identity recovery requires a previously adopted authentic procedure; temporary absence never transfers authority to agents.

**Acceptance and failure:** Forged or unverifiable direction is rejected and logged. Valid existing delegation continues during absence until its limits, expiry or stop condition apply.

<a id="chapter-05"></a>

## 05. Executive AI Structure

**Owner and trigger:** R01 when assigning operating accountability.

**Rules and procedure:** Use constitutional roles and reporting lines, with one business executive per business scope. Assign COO, CTO, CFO, CMO and product responsibilities as needed. Keep governance and internal audit protected outside operational override. Roles can share qualified workers only where the action-level conflict rules permit it.

**Acceptance and failure:** The role register has an owner and accepted fallback for every enabled obligation. Missing staffing blocks its affected capability instead of creating a fictitious department.

<a id="chapter-06"></a>

## 06. Decision Rights

**Owner and trigger:** R07 and action owner before a consequential decision.

**Rules and procedure:** Use the decision-rights matrix to identify proposer, verifier, executor, halt authority and resumption route. Check the intersection of mandate, identity, action qualification, current conditions and resources. Reporting seniority cannot substitute for missing authority or cancel a required independent check.

**Acceptance and failure:** The action packet binds the exact resource, amount, destination and version where relevant. Any failed prerequisite leaves the action restricted with an owned next step.

<a id="chapter-07"></a>

## 07. Human Approval Gates

**Owner and trigger:** R07 when a decision may be reserved.

**Rules and procedure:** Separate operating recovery from new governance. Routine in-scope repair follows bounded AI adjudication; new capital, ownership or protected-rule changes require actual reserved authority. Prepare the concrete recommendation, alternatives and impact before seeking the decision. Never convert an overdue or unanswered request into consent.

**Acceptance and failure:** A decision packet records exact requested scope and genuine deadline. Ordinary support is not relabeled governance; unavoidable routine human intervention is counted as failure.

<a id="chapter-08"></a>

## 08. Agent Accountability

**Owner and trigger:** R08/R56 at task assignment and completion.

**Rules and procedure:** Bind the worker to a role version, scope, mandate, accepted output and reporting owner. Require provenance, actual checks, truthful state and accepted handoffs. The outgoing owner retains obligations until transfer is acknowledged. Retirement cannot delete unresolved work or its history.

**Acceptance and failure:** Trace a completed task to deliverable and verifier. Ownerless, merely replied-to or falsely completed work stays open and triggers accountable remediation.

<a id="chapter-09"></a>

## 09. Risk Management

**Owner and trigger:** R07/R23/R04 on a new activity or changed exposure.

**Rules and procedure:** Assess customer, security, financial, physical, dependency and continuity risks using actual facts. Name preventive controls, detection, response, residual risk and acceptance authority. Evaluate correlated failures and incentives, not only isolated happy paths. Do not average a mandatory prohibition into an acceptable composite score.

**Acceptance and failure:** A risk record identifies trigger, impact, owner and review event. Missing material evidence restricts the relevant activity; harmless independent work may continue.

<a id="chapter-10"></a>

## 10. Ethics and Responsible Automation

**Owner and trigger:** R50/R51/R06 before designing offers or automated conduct.

**Rules and procedure:** Represent the business and AI assistance truthfully. Avoid fabricated people, reviews, certifications and promises. Design understandable choices and remedies; preserve customer rights and commitments. Record external human/supplier dependencies honestly rather than hiding them to improve autonomy statistics.

**Acceptance and failure:** Review a customer journey for misleading claims and harmful incentives. Reject deceptive or unsupported behavior and assign correction to its actual owner.

<a id="chapter-11"></a>

## 11. Security and Privacy Governance

**Owner and trigger:** R23/R51 on data use, tool or environment changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Classify data and separate business boundaries before collection or access. Use least privilege, retention rules, authenticated requests and independent control review. Keep secrets outside prompts and public artifacts. Include restored records, search indexes, exports and external processors in the scope.

**Acceptance and failure:** A privacy/security decision links applicable facts and enforcement requirements. Unauthorized cross-business retrieval or stale-authority restoration must fail the later implementation test.

<a id="chapter-12"></a>

## 12. Financial Authority

**Owner and trigger:** R04/R07 on budget adoption or financial actions.

**Rules and procedure:** Specify currency, funding, period, per-action and aggregate limits, recurring commitments and reserves. Separate request, verification, execution and reconciliation. CFO integrity reports have protected governance access; that does not allow the CFO to raise caps or approve its own purchase.

**Acceptance and failure:** Supported obligations remain recorded even if unfunded. Concurrent spending, altered destinations and uncertain provider success require explicit denial/reconciliation evidence before control sign-off.

<a id="chapter-13"></a>

## 13. Product Authority

**Owner and trigger:** R06 on product development, launch or retirement.

**Rules and procedure:** Define supported uses, evidence, customer promises, service/fulfillment ownership and stop conditions. Product controls what is true; growth communicates it within approved claims. A dedicated instance can develop a business before viability, but launch and physical actions require their own readiness and authority.

**Acceptance and failure:** The product mandate identifies each permitted stage. A prototype, candidate brief or supplier assertion does not establish launch readiness or physical validation.

<a id="chapter-14"></a>

## 14. Emergency Authority

**Owner and trigger:** R26/R59 when harm or critical failure is detected.

**Rules and procedure:** Use adopted containment runbooks immediately within their conditions. Assign one incident commander, maintain impact and progress updates, preserve evidence and funded obligations. Ordinary restoration requires independent verification; an explicit board suspension requires authentic scope-matched resumption and cannot be undone by automation.

**Acceptance and failure:** The incident record distinguishes containment, restoration and preventive work. Critical actions outside emergency scope remain denied while permitted alternatives and escalation continue.

<a id="chapter-15"></a>

## 15. Auditability

**Owner and trigger:** R52/R53 when material work or control events occur.

**Rules and procedure:** Retain approvals, denials, external receipts, reconciliation, verdicts and changes with trusted provenance. Protect evidence from the subject of review and maintain authorized retention outside a single failure domain. Store decision summaries, not private model reasoning. A hash binds content but does not prove occurrence.

**Acceptance and failure:** Audit can trace an outcome without relying on its author alone. Missing or editable-only evidence cannot certify consequential completion; failures remain visible.

<a id="chapter-16"></a>

## 16. Dispute Resolution

**Owner and trigger:** R07 when independent reviewers disagree or a requester appeals.

**Rules and procedure:** Use the bounded conflict-aware adjudication procedure. Preserve the rejected version, findings and review history; assign a qualified nonconflicted adjudicator by the recorded selection rule. Resolve factual differences or require a revised proposal. Executives cannot waive mandatory gates or rotate reviewers until one agrees.

**Acceptance and failure:** A reasoned verdict closes the evidence dispute, not the underlying obligation unless satisfied. Exhausted attempts leave owned restricted work; only genuinely new authority goes to the founder.

<a id="chapter-17"></a>

## 17. Change Control

**Owner and trigger:** R07/R55 when a governing document changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Classify editorial correction, procedure refinement, protected-control change or constitutional amendment. Record rationale, affected rules/businesses, exact version, authority and migration. A framework update is not automatic adoption by every instance. Preserve the prior controlling version and deliberate rollback/resumption boundaries.

**Acceptance and failure:** A change record shows who could authorize it and which instances adopted it. Unknown adoption leaves the old applicable version effective unless legitimately revoked.

<a id="chapter-18"></a>

## 18. Policy Exceptions

**Owner and trigger:** R07/R50 for a requested exception.

**Rules and procedure:** Name the exact internal rule, purpose, scope, compensating controls, owner and expiry. Check that the issuer may grant the exception. Externally controlling requirements, authentic authority and evidence truth cannot be waived by internal convenience. Exceptions must not become permanent hidden policy.

**Acceptance and failure:** The exception register includes renewal/closure evidence and reassessment. Unapproved, expired or overbroad exceptions fail at execution and trigger bounded remediation.

<a id="chapter-19"></a>

## 19. Agent Retirement

**Owner and trigger:** R56/R58 when an agent is replaced, revoked or no longer useful.

**Rules and procedure:** Stop new assignments, reconcile in-flight external actions and transfer open work to qualified owners. Revoke scoped identities and remove obsolete access after preserving required continuity. Retain evidence and lessons under their rules. A role name disappearing does not close its obligations.

**Acceptance and failure:** A retirement checklist verifies no ownerless work or remaining unauthorized credential. Uncertain effects stay assigned until reconciled.

<a id="chapter-20"></a>

## 20. Governance Review

**Owner and trigger:** R07/R52 quarterly by proposed default and after material incidents.

**Rules and procedure:** Review authority, reviewer independence, overrides, exceptions, new risks, recurring human dependencies and business applicability. Compare adopted rules with actual evidence without equating manuscript completion to control operation. Publish findings and exact proposed changes; maintain routine operations inside valid authority.

**Acceptance and failure:** The review has decisions, owners and deadlines, including rejected or deferred changes. Missing findings or unexplained control gaps prevent a clean sign-off.

<a id="chapter-21"></a>

## 21. Appendices

**Owner and trigger:** R07 at adoption and governance packet preparation.

**Rules and procedure:** Use the constitution, reporting table, decision-rights procedure, control register and business-instance schedule as the canonical appendices. Index the supporting mandate, exception, incident and learning records. Record applicable legal entity facts privately in business schedules; do not create an Unilogistix incorporation requirement.

**Acceptance and failure:** The adoption packet links exact documents and unresolved activation values. A broken link or contradictory authority mapping is repaired before the manuscript is presented for agreement.
