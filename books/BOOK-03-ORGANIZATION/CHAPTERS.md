# Organization & Departments — Complete Chapter Manual

Version: 1.0 | Updated: 2026-09-09 | Status: Complete draft manuscript; adoption and operational activation pending

## Reading and authority

This manual supplies substantive procedures for every chapter in the selected blueprint. The [constitution](../../CONSTITUTION.md) is canonical for all 60 roles, reporting, authority and protected oversight. “Company” means the relevant actual business; Unilogistix itself is the reusable framework. LondonRue towels is an example, not an inferred formation or launch.

Each chapter names its owner and trigger, required procedure, and acceptance/failure evidence. All work inherits the constitutional mandate, independent review, privacy, bounded recovery and truthful outcome requirements. Numeric limits and real account/entity identities belong to the [business profile](../../templates/business-instance.md); missing required authority restricts the affected action. Documentary completeness does not claim adopted rules or implemented controls. Every operational human ask is a failure and must be recorded without concealing unresolved work.

Follow [operating assurance](../../policies/operating-assurance.md), [watchdog escalation](../../policies/watchdogs-and-repair-alerts.md), [decision rights](../../governance/DECISION_RIGHTS.md) and [business-to-framework learning](../../workflows/business-to-framework-learning.md) where applicable. A real legal/provider requirement is verified for its exact business, action and current source before use; no illustrative company cadence is a statutory deadline.

## 01. Organizational Philosophy

**Owner and trigger:** R01/R07 when configuring a business team.

**Rules and procedure:** Organize around owned outcomes and independent controls, not a permanent model per department. Use one primary reporting owner for each scoped assignment. Assign compatible roles economically while preserving actual recusal, credentials and evidence boundaries for material reviews.

**Acceptance and failure:** The business role map covers enabled work and qualified replacements. A role may be inactive only with documented non-applicability or retained obligation ownership.

<a id="chapter-02"></a>

## 02. Executive Office

**Owner and trigger:** R01 on strategy, coordination and executive review.

**Rules and procedure:** Coordinate COO, CTO, CFO, CMO, product and intelligence within the business mandate. Preserve direct governance/audit access and the CFO integrity route. Framework maintenance reports in its own founder scope, not as a legal parent directing all businesses.

**Acceptance and failure:** A briefing shows business outcomes and unresolved decisions without filtering adverse control evidence. Executive rank cannot reverse a mandatory failure.

<a id="chapter-03"></a>

## 03. Product

**Owner and trigger:** R06/R11 on customer needs and product changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Own supported product truth, requirements, lifecycle priorities and acceptance criteria. Integrate research, customer evidence, supplier constraints and economics. Growth proposes messaging but cannot define an unavailable capability as supported. Product must preserve maintenance and retirement responsibilities.

**Acceptance and failure:** An accepted brief names outcome, scope, testable criteria and fulfillment owner. Unsupported changes remain proposals rather than customer promises.

<a id="chapter-04"></a>

## 04. Engineering

**Owner and trigger:** R03 when product work enters technical delivery.

**Rules and procedure:** Assign architecture and specialist work, dependencies and operational ownership. Deliver focused reviewable changes with required testing, independent acceptance and recovery readiness. Coordinate mobile and hardware only where the business enables them. Quality gates remain protected outside operating pressure.

**Acceptance and failure:** Engineering completion includes versioned deliverable, relevant checks and accepted operational handoff. A working local demo alone is not a production release.

<a id="chapter-05"></a>

## 05. Frontend

**Owner and trigger:** R15 on accepted web or storefront changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Implement R12 designs against real interface contracts, including accessibility, error and empty states. Preserve clear price, consent and order status. Coordinate with backend and independent QA; do not embed secret credentials or invent completed payments.

**Acceptance and failure:** Review and tests cover the changed customer journey. Broken checkout or misleading status blocks release and gets a minimal reproduction.

<a id="chapter-06"></a>

## 06. Backend

**Owner and trigger:** R16 on server or provider integration requirements.

**Rules and procedure:** Define validation, authorization, error handling and duplicate-effect prevention. Keep business state authoritative and reconcile uncertain provider results. Coordinate with R18 data and R60 control boundaries. Backward compatibility and operating diagnostics are part of the delivery.

**Acceptance and failure:** Integration evidence distinguishes mock, sandbox and live scope. Invalid authority and timeout-after-success cases must have a defined safe result.

<a id="chapter-07"></a>

## 07. Architecture

**Owner and trigger:** R14 when interfaces, data or deployment boundaries change.

**Rules and procedure:** Document options and the simplest design meeting acceptance, isolation, cost and recovery needs. Assign one source of truth per entity and make failure dependencies visible. Do not choose new vendors or split services merely because the blueprint lists departments.

**Acceptance and failure:** An architecture decision records tradeoffs and compatibility obligations. Undefined ownership or direct privileged bypass prevents acceptance.

<a id="chapter-08"></a>

## 08. QA

**Owner and trigger:** R21/R22 at requirement review and candidate validation.

**Rules and procedure:** Derive meaningful risk coverage independently of the implementation. Builders may write tests; protected acceptance cannot be authored and graded solely by the builder. Keep exact artifact/environment versions, fixtures and actual run results.

**Acceptance and failure:** Failures, skips and flakes remain explicit. A revised test expectation needs justified independent review before it can unblock a release.

<a id="chapter-09"></a>

## 09. Security

**Owner and trigger:** R23/R60 on threats, access or control changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Separate security assurance from implementation. Technical workers repair; independent assurance evaluates boundaries and can hold affected actions. Identity administration grants only authentic scope and cannot approve changes to its own protection. Audit may examine both.

**Acceptance and failure:** Vulnerability findings have owners and independent closure evidence. A worker cannot obtain broader credentials through an optimistic prompt or executive instruction.

<a id="chapter-10"></a>

## 10. DevOps / SRE

**Owner and trigger:** R24/R25/R26 when services are built, released or impaired.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain reproducible infrastructure, protected delivery and customer-path reliability. Plan capacity and renewal within adopted limits; test restoration and incident response. Independent watchdogs detect missing work and failed delivery even if the primary host is unavailable.

**Acceptance and failure:** Release and repair records verify actual outcomes. Do not call a backup file, local timer or deployment command complete service recovery.

<a id="chapter-11"></a>

## 11. Data

**Owner and trigger:** R18/R51 when defining or moving data.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain schemas, lineage, integrity, access and retention in the correct business scope. Coordinate source stores with derived analytics/search without creating competing truths. Test migrations and restoration with current restrictions reapplied.

**Acceptance and failure:** Validation reconciles source and target state. Privacy holds, deletion restrictions and customer boundaries survive retries, exports and restore.

<a id="chapter-12"></a>

## 12. AI / ML

**Owner and trigger:** R19/R56 when a model-based capability is justified.

**Rules and procedure:** Select and configure models using explicit quality, privacy, latency and cost criteria. Register evaluated versions and narrow tool access. Independent R57 evaluates promotions and harmful regressions; a new model does not inherit authority by name.

**Acceptance and failure:** Qualification records identify action and environment. Unsupported training data or failed denial tests keep the capability inactive.

<a id="chapter-13"></a>

## 13. UX / UI

**Owner and trigger:** R12 on customer-interface specification.

**Rules and procedure:** Design understandable task flows, keyboard/device accessibility and honest failure recovery. Include mobile and weak-network states where relevant. Validate designs against actual supported behavior; avoid inactive controls that suggest an action completed.

**Acceptance and failure:** Usability findings tie to tasks and affected users. Critical confusion or inaccessible paths become owned requirements before launch.

<a id="chapter-14"></a>

## 14. Research

**Owner and trigger:** R09/R10 on uncertainty or market opportunity.

**Rules and procedure:** Research customers, alternatives, supplier feasibility and economics with dated sources and counterevidence. Product owns requirements; growth receives permitted audience insight. Separate observation, assumption and recommendation, and constrain resource-intensive investigation.

**Acceptance and failure:** A research packet changes or validates a decision. Unsupported certainty and fabricated customer interviews fail acceptance.

<a id="chapter-15"></a>

## 15. Marketing

**Owner and trigger:** R05/R27 on acquisition and retention goals.

**Rules and procedure:** Run the documented research-to-customer-outcome loop with approved claims, offers, audiences and resource limits. Coordinate sales, onboarding, finance and support capacity. Preserve unsuccessful campaigns as learning rather than hiding them from reports.

**Acceptance and failure:** Campaign acceptance includes verified distribution and meaningful outcomes. Acquisition pauses when applicable service or financial guardrails fail.

<a id="chapter-16"></a>

## 16. Brand

**Owner and trigger:** R13 on brand assets or positioning changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain business-specific identity, evidence-backed product presentation and rights to creative assets. Keep shared framework templates separate from a business brand. Version claims and flag dependent assets when product facts change.

**Acceptance and failure:** A brand packet has source/rights records and correct supported claims. Invented endorsements or misleading product images are rejected.

<a id="chapter-17"></a>

## 17. Social Media

**Owner and trigger:** R29 on approved channel activity and responses.

**Rules and procedure:** Publish through authorized business accounts, confirm external results and route customer cases to accepted owners. Honor current platform requirements and scoped communications. Never manufacture engagement or bypass restrictions using substitute identities.

**Acceptance and failure:** Publication receipts and response handoffs support completion. Failed account access becomes remediation, not invisible abandoned work.

<a id="chapter-18"></a>

## 18. Advertising

**Owner and trigger:** R30 on adopted paid-channel experiments.

**Rules and procedure:** Reserve exposure, include reporting delays and fees, then optimize only inside permitted channels and limits. Reconcile external campaign state and stop new exposure when spend or service evidence is unreliable. Do not assume a provider setting is a guaranteed cap.

**Acceptance and failure:** Financial and attribution records reconcile to receipts and cohorts. Over-limit or unknown exposure prevents additional commitments.

<a id="chapter-19"></a>

## 19. Sales

**Owner and trigger:** R32 on eligible leads and buyer requests.

**Rules and procedure:** Qualify the need and use supported offers, accurate assurance packets and standard terms. Distinguish verified facts, commitments, proposals and unknowns. Route deviations through legal and actual authority; do not create unsupported guarantees.

**Acceptance and failure:** A sale closes only with accepted terms and an acknowledged delivery/onboarding obligation. A sent quotation is not collected revenue.

<a id="chapter-20"></a>

## 20. CRM

**Owner and trigger:** R33 on lead, identity and lifecycle events.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain canonical identity and scoped contact preferences while linking commercial, service and financial dimensions to their authoritative owners. CMO controls opportunities, COO service, and finance payment records; departments do not independently invent customer truth.

**Acceptance and failure:** Deduplication and preference changes have evidence. Weak identity matching or conflicting records block sensitive disclosure until resolved.

<a id="chapter-21"></a>

## 21. Customer Success

**Owner and trigger:** R34 on customer activation and continued-value signals.

**Rules and procedure:** Own first useful outcomes, onboarding commitments and permitted recovery for stalled customers. For goods, distinguish purchase from verified fulfillment; do not invent satisfaction for nonresponders. Feed supported needs to product and promises to accountable owners.

**Acceptance and failure:** Customer success is based on actual useful outcome evidence. Unfinished onboarding remains owned rather than disappearing because no ticket was filed.

<a id="chapter-22"></a>

## 22. Customer Support

**Owner and trigger:** R35 on customer problems and complaints.

**Rules and procedure:** Authenticate sensitive requests, classify impact and diagnose from evidence. Deliver an authorized remedy or an accepted escalation with preserved obligation. Coordinate incidents, returns, billing and product learning; a response alone does not resolve the case.

**Acceptance and failure:** Closure links verified remedy and customer follow-through. Repeated problems feed root-cause work and remain visible in quality metrics.

<a id="chapter-23"></a>

## 23. Chatbot Operations

**Owner and trigger:** R36 on business conversations.

**Rules and procedure:** Use approved knowledge and scoped account context to answer accurately and invoke narrow permitted actions. Preserve uncertainty and hand off unsupported cases. Conversation text cannot alter budgets, recipients or identity controls.

**Acceptance and failure:** Evaluate factual answers, denied actions and accepted transfers. Invented refunds, human staff or shipment status are failures, not helpful personalization.

<a id="chapter-24"></a>

## 24. Finance

**Owner and trigger:** R04/R43/R47 on plans and funding requests.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain resources, liquidity, reserves and exposure per actual business. Execute only independently verified, scoped actions; preserve protected financial-integrity reporting. Shared framework services do not create permission to pool money or raise caps.

**Acceptance and failure:** Forecast, cash, liabilities and reservations remain separate. Missing funding blocks execution while leaving genuine obligations recorded.

<a id="chapter-25"></a>

## 25. Accounting

**Owner and trigger:** R44/R45/R46/R48/R49 on transaction and close cycles.

**Rules and procedure:** Keep supported books, payable/receivable records, independent bank/provider reconciliation and business-specific tax preparation. Review adjustments separately from their author. Preserve period-close history and applicable record requirements.

**Acceptance and failure:** Invoices, receipts and actual statements substantiate balances. Unexplained differences stay open; changing a ledger to hide a discrepancy is prohibited.

<a id="chapter-26"></a>

## 26. Legal / Compliance

**Owner and trigger:** R50/R07/R51 on new activities, terms and obligations.

**Rules and procedure:** Identify jurisdiction- and entity-specific duties using current authoritative sources. Prepare contract/applicability records and required authentic signatures. Distinguish internal AI responsibilities from legal officer status or professional credentials. Track unresolved matters without inventing compliance.

**Acceptance and failure:** A sourced register names owner, triggering facts and deadlines. A missing legal prerequisite restricts its activity, not the reusable manuscript itself.

<a id="chapter-27"></a>

## 27. Operations

**Owner and trigger:** R02/R37–R41 on orders and recurring business work.

**Rules and procedure:** Coordinate suppliers, stock, fulfillment, quality, remedies and renewal obligations under one accountable operating plan. Confirm external physical evidence and retain failures. Scale acquisition to supported delivery and resources rather than appearances.

**Acceptance and failure:** LondonRue order exceptions connect to stock, supplier, money and support owners. No unfulfilled obligation becomes complete when an API merely accepts a request.

<a id="chapter-28"></a>

## 28. Business Intelligence

**Owner and trigger:** R54 when managers need performance evidence.

**Rules and procedure:** Define metrics from authoritative events with windows, exclusions, freshness and uncertainty. Reconcile finance and customer outcomes without overlapping attribution. Provide direct discrepancy reporting that cannot be suppressed by the manager whose metrics are adverse.

**Acceptance and failure:** A sample report reproduces from source records. Missing data, stale evidence and zero denominators remain visible as unavailable.

<a id="chapter-29"></a>

## 29. Project / Program Management

**Owner and trigger:** R08 on cross-role plans and delivery changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain dependency graph, owners, realistic due dates and scope-change impact. Use accepted handoffs and shared operation limits. Resolve resource contention through the business scheduler rather than assigning multiple agents to conflicting repairs.

**Acceptance and failure:** Every blocking dependency has an owner and next action. Reporting task volume without verified completion is insufficient.

<a id="chapter-30"></a>

## 30. Internal Audit

**Owner and trigger:** R52 on risk-based reviews and adverse evidence.

**Rules and procedure:** Audit operating and assurance performance through protected source access and direct governance reporting. Do not execute transactions or routine approvals being audited. Preserve findings and independently review material audit conclusions.

**Acceptance and failure:** Subjects cannot edit or suppress findings or certify their own remediation. Overdue corrective action stays visible until independently closed.

<a id="chapter-31"></a>

## 31. Knowledge Management

**Owner and trigger:** R53 on lessons, documents and source changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain canonical knowledge, versioning, provenance and scoped reuse. Record disposition of meaningful outcomes, including rejected lessons. Promote permitted generalizations through framework evaluation; do not clone private operational memory.

**Acceptance and failure:** Retrieval and documentation remain current and attributable. Stale or conflicting guidance is restricted pending validation.

<a id="chapter-32"></a>

## 32. Department Interfaces

**Owner and trigger:** R08/R02 when work crosses departments.

**Rules and procedure:** Define sender, receiver, authoritative records, acceptance test, due time and failure route for each interface. Use references rather than copying secrets or duplicating source truth. The sender remains accountable until explicit receiver acceptance.

**Acceptance and failure:** A rejected handoff leaves a visible obligation with its owner. Lost messages or duplicate events cannot silently transfer or erase responsibility.

<a id="chapter-33"></a>

## 33. Department KPIs

**Owner and trigger:** R54 with each department lead at profile adoption.

**Rules and procedure:** Set outcome metrics, formula, source, cohort, target and breach response. Pair speed and cost with quality, customer impact and unresolved work. Include human asks honestly and avoid incentives to split tasks or hide failures.

**Acceptance and failure:** Metric definitions are versioned and reproducible. An unmeasured or zero-activity department cannot claim perfect autonomy.

<a id="chapter-34"></a>

## 34. Escalation Paths

**Owner and trigger:** R07/R26/R59 when ordinary recovery or deadlines fail.

**Rules and procedure:** Use watchdog severity and the bounded independent adjudication route. Managers repair and prioritize; protected acceptance remains intact. Founder requests are reserved for actual new authority, not a default destination for disagreement.

**Acceptance and failure:** Escalation has owner, evidence and deadline. Unanswered requests do not authorize action; unavoidable routine human help is recorded as failure.
