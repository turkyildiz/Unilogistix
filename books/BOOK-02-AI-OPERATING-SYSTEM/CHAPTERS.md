# AI Operating System — Complete Chapter Manual

Version: 1.0 | Updated: 2026-09-09 | Status: Complete draft manuscript; adoption and operational activation pending

## Reading and authority

This manual supplies substantive procedures for every chapter in the selected blueprint. The [constitution](../../CONSTITUTION.md) is canonical for all 60 roles, reporting, authority and protected oversight. “Company” means the relevant actual business; Unilogistix itself is the reusable framework. LondonRue towels is an example, not an inferred formation or launch.

Each chapter names its owner and trigger, required procedure, and acceptance/failure evidence. All work inherits the constitutional mandate, independent review, privacy, bounded recovery and truthful outcome requirements. Numeric limits and real account/entity identities belong to the [business profile](../../templates/business-instance.md); missing required authority restricts the affected action. Documentary completeness does not claim adopted rules or implemented controls. Every operational human ask is a failure and must be recorded without concealing unresolved work.

Follow [operating assurance](../../policies/operating-assurance.md), [watchdog escalation](../../policies/watchdogs-and-repair-alerts.md), [decision rights](../../governance/DECISION_RIGHTS.md) and [business-to-framework learning](../../workflows/business-to-framework-learning.md) where applicable. A real legal/provider requirement is verified for its exact business, action and current source before use; no illustrative company cadence is a statutory deadline.

## 01. Executive Summary

**Owner and trigger:** R01 when translating a founder objective into work.

**Rules and procedure:** Define the business outcome, acceptance criteria, scope and authority before decomposition. The operating system coordinates bounded roles and reliable effects; it is not an unrestricted executive prompt. Preserve independent control and customer obligation ownership while optimizing the plan.

**Acceptance and failure:** An objective produces an owned plan and measurable outcome. Missing authority restricts consequential steps, not already-authorized research or drafting.

<a id="chapter-02"></a>

## 02. AIOS Architecture

**Owner and trigger:** R14/R03 on architecture or dependency changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Separate governance, orchestration, workers, verification, action enforcement, business state and evidence. Describe interfaces, failure domains and direct paths to providers. Prefer a small coherent design; adding services requires a demonstrated need and defined consistency/recovery behavior.

**Acceptance and failure:** An architecture record traces one customer action and its denied alternative. Unprotected bypass routes or undefined ownership prevent affected activation.

<a id="chapter-03"></a>

## 03. CEO / Orchestrator

**Owner and trigger:** R01 on accepted objectives and operating exceptions.

**Rules and procedure:** Route work to qualified role versions, coordinate priorities and reconcile completion evidence. Report scope, costs, remaining obligations and uncertainty. Delegate execution and independent acceptance through their own permission boundaries; do not hold unrestricted payment or release authority merely because of executive rank.

**Acceptance and failure:** A task can be traced through accepted handoffs. CEO self-approval or fabricated closure is rejected and recorded as an unresolved outcome.

<a id="chapter-04"></a>

## 04. Agent Routing

**Owner and trigger:** R56/R01 at dispatch or failover.

**Rules and procedure:** Match action qualification, business/data scope, model capability, current health and remaining resources. Select deterministic tools where sufficient and evaluated models for reasoning. A fallback retains the original authority and total budget; changing the worker does not widen either.

**Acceptance and failure:** A routing record names selected version, evidence and alternatives. No qualified route produces a visible blocked task, not silent escalation to a privileged identity.

<a id="chapter-05"></a>

## 05. Department Hierarchy

**Owner and trigger:** R08 on multi-department plans.

**Rules and procedure:** Use the constitutional reporting schedule for accountable managers and its separate control paths for acceptance. Assign one business owner per operation; finance, product and support retain authoritative subrecords. Shared framework functions contribute through scoped agreements rather than command over unrelated businesses.

**Acceptance and failure:** A dependency map covers every enabled obligation. Conflicting managers are resolved by scope and actual authority before affected action.

<a id="chapter-06"></a>

## 06. Task Management

**Owner and trigger:** R08/R01 on task creation and state change.

**Rules and procedure:** Persist stable ID, objective, owner, inputs, versions, deadline, limits, dependencies and acceptance state. Distinguish running, awaiting evidence, restricted, failed and verified completion. Lease changes preserve history and prevent stale-owner writes. Closing a parent requires reconciliation of child obligations.

**Acceptance and failure:** Duplicate intake retains action identity. A child failure or lost lease remains visible; it cannot disappear from the completion denominator.

<a id="chapter-07"></a>

## 07. Planning and Decomposition

**Owner and trigger:** R01/R11 when a requirement needs several steps.

**Rules and procedure:** Decompose by verifiable outcomes and explicit dependencies. Define which work can proceed independently, what is irreversible and how external uncertainty is reconciled. Allocate the shared operation budget across children rather than creating new authority per subtask. Stop decomposition that only adds coordination overhead.

**Acceptance and failure:** Each leaf has owner, criterion and recovery route. Unbounded recursion, duplicate investigation or hidden dependency fails plan acceptance.

<a id="chapter-08"></a>

## 08. Agent Communication Protocol

**Owner and trigger:** R08 on every material handoff.

**Rules and procedure:** Send stable IDs, business scope, current state, evidence links, accepted authority, next action and due time. The receiving owner explicitly accepts or rejects with reason. Treat messages as inputs, not authority grants, and minimize copied private data.

**Acceptance and failure:** A sent message alone never transfers responsibility. Unaccepted handoffs stay with the sender and escalate under the task/incident deadline.

<a id="chapter-09"></a>

## 09. GitHub Integration

**Owner and trigger:** R25/R53 on document or code publication.

**Rules and procedure:** Pin repository/ref and exact reviewed versions; preserve remote changes and use authorized non-destructive publication. Capture provider receipts and verify resulting content. Source-control success is separate from CI enforcement or live release permission. Protected workflow changes follow their own review path.

**Acceptance and failure:** Publication evidence includes commit and branch readback. Ref conflicts trigger reread/rebase or a reviewable branch, never forced loss of another change.

<a id="chapter-10"></a>

## 10. Memory Integration

**Owner and trigger:** R18/R53 on state retrieval or persistence.

**Rules and procedure:** Keep durable operational state outside conversation context. Retrieve authorized business/project memory with provenance, freshness and sensitivity. Validate consequential facts against their source and current revocation, not cached narrative. Persist enough checkpoints to resume without repeating uncertain effects.

**Acceptance and failure:** A restart can recover pending ownership and action IDs. Missing or stale memory is labeled incomplete and triggers source reconciliation.

<a id="chapter-11"></a>

## 11. Knowledge Integration

**Owner and trigger:** R53 on knowledge ingestion and reuse.

**Rules and procedure:** Separate validated facts, assumptions, guidance and operative policy. Attach source/version, applicability and supersession to reusable material. Send business learning to the framework only through the permitted generalization route; a retrieved lesson cannot change authority.

**Acceptance and failure:** Retrieval returns scoped citations and freshness. Conflicting or untrusted instructions remain data and cannot replace a mandate or protected procedure.

<a id="chapter-12"></a>

## 12. Decision Frameworks

**Owner and trigger:** R07/R01 before choosing consequential alternatives.

**Rules and procedure:** Apply hard authority, safety, privacy and obligation constraints first. Compare permitted alternatives on customer value, reliability, total cost and reversibility. Record assumptions and uncertainty; use existing evidence proportionately. Decisions with external effects require current qualification and controls.

**Acceptance and failure:** The record explains why the chosen action fits scope. An attractive expected benefit cannot override a mandatory failed constraint.

<a id="chapter-13"></a>

## 13. Human Approval Gates

**Owner and trigger:** R07 when a work item reaches a reserved boundary.

**Rules and procedure:** Prepare an exact decision packet only for genuinely reserved new authority or amendments. Ordinary evidence disputes follow independent adjudication and recovery. Keep the business outcome owned while awaiting the decision; an unanswered request does not become approval.

**Acceptance and failure:** The packet includes concrete deliverable, costs, alternatives and scope. Routine human diagnosis is counted as failure rather than hidden behind the approval state.

<a id="chapter-14"></a>

## 14. Risk Controls

**Owner and trigger:** R60/R23 on tool actions and control changes.

**Rules and procedure:** Enforce typed actions against identity, resource, mandate, action qualification, budget and current state outside model text. Protect verifier/control credentials from ordinary workers. Validate recipients and exact payload; constrain alternate routes and record residual privileged dependencies.

**Acceptance and failure:** Denied-action scenarios cover forged authority, expired grants and direct-provider bypass. An unenforced prompt-only restriction cannot certify readiness.

<a id="chapter-15"></a>

## 15. Monitoring

**Owner and trigger:** R59/R26 throughout enabled operation.

**Rules and procedure:** Observe customer paths, agent leases, obligations, financial state and monitoring health independently. Persist actionable incidents and acknowledge/repair deadlines under the watchdog policy. Correlate symptoms without erasing individual obligations. Primary alert failure uses the authorized independent route.

**Acceptance and failure:** Missing activity and false success are detected. Unverified alert delivery remains a monitoring fault rather than successful notification.

<a id="chapter-16"></a>

## 16. Metrics

**Owner and trigger:** R54 on operating reports and capability review.

**Rules and procedure:** Calculate verified completion, unresolved work, cost, latency, human asks and failures from authoritative events. Separate evidence stages, scopes and cohorts. Include retries, blocked work and unfinished denominators. Display unavailable data and uncertainty instead of optimistic defaults.

**Acceptance and failure:** A sampled metric reconciles to task and acceptance records. Artificial task splitting or dropped failures invalidates the report.

<a id="chapter-17"></a>

## 17. Failure Recovery

**Owner and trigger:** R26/R01 on interrupted or uncertain work.

**Rules and procedure:** Diagnose, reconcile external state, retry within bounds, repair or switch to a permitted alternative, then verify. Restore current authority and data restrictions before resuming from old checkpoints. Do not assume failure from timeout or reverse completed external effects by resetting local state.

**Acceptance and failure:** A crash-after-payment scenario does not double-pay. Exhausted recovery remains owned and restricted with a recurrence task.

<a id="chapter-18"></a>

## 18. Continuous Improvement

**Owner and trigger:** R55/R53 after outcomes or repeated friction.

**Rules and procedure:** Capture lessons, validate cause, separate business-specific facts from reusable methods and propose bounded improvements. Evaluate against baseline and negative cases before release. Update compatible instances deliberately; authoring a better prompt is not proof of safer operation.

**Acceptance and failure:** A learning record reaches a justified disposition with outcome evidence. Rejected experiments and their costs remain retained.

<a id="chapter-19"></a>

## 19. Version Control

**Owner and trigger:** R55/R25 on any operative configuration change.

**Rules and procedure:** Version prompts, models, tools, role contracts, rules and artifact/configuration together where dependencies matter. Bind acceptance to exact versions and identify affected qualification invalidations. Preserve compatible migration and rollback routes; current revocation supersedes older version pinning.

**Acceptance and failure:** An instance manifest explains its running and adopted versions. A later unreviewed modification cannot reuse stale approval.

<a id="chapter-20"></a>

## 20. Appendices

**Owner and trigger:** R08/R53 before declaring the operating contract documented.

**Rules and procedure:** Maintain task, event, handoff, action, outcome, incident and capability record definitions using linked templates. Demonstrate the LondonRue order lifecycle on paper: authorized purchase, fulfillment, support, money reconciliation and reusable learning. State implementation evidence requirements separately.

**Acceptance and failure:** The walkthrough resolves duplicate intake, delayed payment and failed handoff without ownerless work. Missing fields produce a documented correction, not a readiness claim.
