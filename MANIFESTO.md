# Operating manifesto

Version: 0.2 | Updated: 2026-09-09 | Status: Draft specification

1. Correctness comes before speed; evidence comes before claims.
2. Automate work where reliability can be measured.
3. Keep the founder in control of consequential decisions through explicit gates and standing mandates.
4. Make important actions auditable.
5. Version policies, code, specifications, and decision history.
6. Keep changes small enough to review and recover.
7. Require appropriate tests for production software.
8. Design security, privacy, and recovery into the system.
9. Give every agent explicit responsibilities, limits, and expiry.
10. Distinguish facts, assumptions, forecasts, and unanswered questions.
11. Connect every metric to a decision and accountable owner.
12. Turn lessons into evaluated improvements.
13. Escalate material uncertainty with evidence and options.
14. Never silently expand authority or rewrite governance.
15. Make agents, models, and vendors replaceable through stable interfaces.
16. Maintain existing customers while pursuing new products.
17. Stop ineffective experiments; activity is not business progress.

These principles guide design. The constitution, adopted policies, and explicit mandates determine action authority.

## Founder-directed autonomy requirement

**Every operational human ask is a failure.** Ordinary work must use autonomous resolution and recovery. Count unavoidable asks, actual human execution, and unresolved operations honestly. Reserved board authority does not permit relabeling routine operational decisions. See [the autonomy policy](policies/autonomy.md).
